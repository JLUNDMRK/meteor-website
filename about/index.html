About Meteor

Independent movie-library application for Android, iPhone, and Apple TV.

Why Meteor?

I built Meteor because I wanted a simple way to browse my own movie collection without unnecessary complexity.

Modern streaming services make discovering movies effortless. I wanted the same experience for media I already own.

Meteor is an independent project created and maintained by a single developer.

The goal is to make personal movie libraries easy to browse and enjoy, without requiring a web server on your NAS, a Meteor account, or unnecessary configuration.

Meteor focuses primarily on indexing, movie identification, metadata and library navigation. Playback uses platform media stacks plus optional open-source libraries described below.

Install. Index. Enjoy.

Codecs & playback

This version of Meteor plays with ExoPlayer (Media3) using your device’s built-in media support. Some files may not play if the device cannot decode them natively.

When needed, Meteor can open the VLC app so you can watch those titles there.

Your media & metadata

Meteor provides no movie files, images, or metadata of its own. You accept responsibility for OMDb API data (and other metadata providers) that you use, and for any movies or files you copy or download locally for use with the app.

Open source & licenses

Meteor may include or link open-source components. Attribution also appears in the app under Settings → Other / About.

FFmpeg (LGPL)

Optional decode-only FFmpeg builds are licensed under the GNU LGPL v2.1+ (no encoders; no GPL/nonfree components in the freedom profile). See ffmpeg.org. Hardware codecs such as H.264/HEVC on the device OS are separate from FFmpeg.

VLC / VLCKit

Apple (iPhone & Apple TV): Meteor can embed MobileVLCKit / TVVLCKit under LGPLv2.1+ as a dynamic framework (“Powered by VLC”). This is the VideoLAN VLCKit library — not the GPL “VLC for iOS” application, and not GPL-only plugins. Source: VideoLAN VLCKit. Users may replace the LGPL library; App Store builds include an object-file / relink offer on request (same policy as FFmpeg).

Android: This version uses ExoPlayer (Media3) with device codecs. Meteor does not ship or embed a VLC binary. When needed, Meteor may open the external VLC app if the user has installed it.

VLC and VideoLAN are trademarks of VideoLAN. Project site: videolan.org.

Support the Project

The app is free to use for now.

If you'd like to support its development, you can choose an optional low-cost Supporter subscription.

The Supporter subscription does not currently unlock any additional features. It exists for people who want to support the project and help fund its continued development.

My goal is to keep the app affordable and accessible for everyone. To help keep the project sustainable, some features may require a Supporter subscription in the future.

There are no guarantees about which features may be added in the future or which features may require a Supporter subscription.

The Supporter subscription is currently entirely optional, and you can cancel it at any time.

The aim is to keep the core functionality of the app free while keeping the Supporter subscription affordable.

Features, pricing, metadata access and supported services may change as the project evolves. No permanent availability of any particular third-party service is promised.
