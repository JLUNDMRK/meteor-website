1. What Meteor is

Meteor is an independent movie-library app for Android phones, tablets, and Android TV / Google TV. It helps you connect to movie collections on a PC or NAS via DLNA, SMB, or WebDAV (including carefully configured remote WebDAV when you choose that), index a library on your device, optionally download individual media files for offline playback, identify titles using third-party metadata services where configured, and browse and play movies using your device’s media player.

Meteor does not require a Meteor account and does not provide a Meteor-hosted library of movie files. Meteor does not provide movie files, images, or metadata of its own. Library media and related data that you browse, index, stream, or download come from sources you connect (your servers and shares) and from third-party metadata services configured for the App — currently the OMDb API for title identification, and Wikidata / Wikimedia-related sources for artwork when used.

You are responsible for ensuring you have the rights to access, use, copy, and store that media and related data. Meteor cannot technically determine whether you have such rights and does not attempt to verify them. Where films include age ratings or similar labels in metadata, Meteor displays that information as supplied by your library data or by those third-party sources. Meteor cannot be held responsible if a rating is incorrect or missing — the App only mediates between data you supply or third-party providers return, and you as the user.

Meteor relies on the OMDb API for accurate titles and metadata. Users are encouraged to support OMDb — for example by contributing to the project or by using their own API key where that option is offered. Homepage: omdbapi.com.

3. Playback and Download

Playback depends substantially on your device and platform media stack, including hardware-supported decoding when available. Meteor does not ship its own broad codec package. Some movies may not play in-app if the device cannot decode them.

We recommend installing the VLC app as a fallback. Meteor can open it for titles the built-in player cannot handle. VLC is a separate application from VideoLAN.

Download in Meteor copies media you select onto the device for offline playback. You remain responsible for that content and for storage space on the device.
