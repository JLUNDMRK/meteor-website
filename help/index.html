Help

Common questions first. Advanced networking (remote WebDAV, CGNAT, port forwarding) is grouped at the end — you can ignore it for a normal home setup.

Does Meteor require an account?

No. Meteor does not require a Meteor account to browse or play your library. You may still need accounts for third-party services you choose to use elsewhere (for example your NAS login or Google Play).

DLNA, WebDAV, or SMB — which should I use?

Not sure? Start with DLNA on your home network. Use SMB if you already have shared folders or want reliable sidecar subtitles. Use WebDAV for similar folder access; treat Internet-facing WebDAV as advanced.

Full comparison and setup: Set up media access.

Why can’t Meteor find my media server?

Local discovery often fails for network reasons. Check the following:

  • Your device and the PC/NAS must be on the same local network.
  • DLNA, SMB, or WebDAV must be enabled for the folders you want.
  • A video / movie folder must actually be shared.
  • VPN software on the phone can interfere with local discovery — try disabling it temporarily.
  • Router client isolation (AP isolation / guest Wi‑Fi) can prevent devices from seeing each other.
  • Firewalls on the NAS, PC, or router may block local discovery or file sharing.

Protocol-specific tips: DLNA · SMB · WebDAV. If discovery still fails after these checks, see Contact.

SMB login fails
  • Confirm username/password and that the user can read the share.
  • Try the device’s local IP instead of a name if name resolution is unreliable.
  • Check whether the NAS restricts SMB versions (SMB2/SMB3).
  • Do not try to reach SMB over the public Internet — use home Wi‑Fi, a VPN, or another approach.

Setup outline: Set up SMB.

Can I watch away from home without remote networking?

Yes. At home, connect with DLNA or SMB (or local WebDAV), then use Download in Meteor for titles you want offline. See Download as an easy alternative.

Why was a movie identified incorrectly?

Identification uses filenames and folder names plus third-party metadata services. Ambiguous names, missing years, or incomplete provider data can produce the wrong match. Choose the correct title with a manual search in the app. If no good result appears, the movie may not be in the metadata provider’s database. See also Metadata & Attribution.

Which formats are supported?

Playback depends substantially on your device and platform media stack (including hardware decoding when available). Meteor does not ship its own broad codec package.

Some files may not play if the device cannot decode them. We recommend installing the excellent VLC app as a fallback — it can play almost anything, and Meteor can open it for titles the built-in player cannot handle.

See also About.

Does Meteor upload my movies?

Meteor is designed so that your movie files stay on your PC or NAS (or on the device if you use Download). Meteor does not upload your movie files to a Meteor server (there is no Meteor media backend for storing your library).

When movie identification runs, selected search terms (such as title and year derived from filenames) may be sent from your device to third-party metadata providers. See the Privacy Policy for details.

Does Meteor need an Internet connection?

Local discovery and playback from your home network do not require Internet access. An Internet connection is typically needed to identify movies and download posters and descriptive metadata from third-party providers, and for Firebase diagnostics / Play purchases when those features run. Downloaded titles can play offline.

Is Meteor free?

Base functions are free to use. Some features are reserved for a local 90-day trial and then for active supporters (a low tip-jar subscription via Google Play). My hope is that users who like the app will contribute so I can keep developing it.

My goal is to keep it affordable and accessible. Features and pricing may change; see the Terms of Use and About.

How do I report a problem?

Use the support email on the Contact page. Include your device model, OS version, media server type if known, and what you expected versus what happened.

Advanced: remote access troubleshooting

Only relevant if you are publishing WebDAV (or similar) to the Internet. Prefer Download when you only need offline playback. Full setup sequence: Remote WebDAV.

WebDAV works at home but not away
  • Confirm HTTPS URL, hostname, and port match what you forwarded.
  • Test on mobile data with home Wi‑Fi off.
  • Check router port forwarding / firewall and NAS firewall.
  • Verify DDNS points at your current public IP.
  • On Synology, do not use a QuickConnect URL for WebDAV.
DDNS hostname looks wrong
  • Check the DDNS status page on the NAS/router — is the recorded IP current?
  • Wait for DNS TTL; try again after a few minutes.
  • Compare with a “what is my IP” result from inside the home.
Port forward seems set but nothing connects
  • Forward to the NAS local IP and the correct internal WebDAV port.
  • Give the NAS a DHCP reservation so the IP does not change.
  • Confirm the router WAN address is actually reachable (see public IP / NAT below).
  • Disable conflicting UPnP rules or duplicate forwards for the same port.
Public IP, double NAT, or CGNAT?

Compare the router’s WAN IPv4 with an external “what is my IP” result. Matching addresses suggest your router holds the public IPv4. Different addresses mean another NAT layer — which may be CGNAT, double NAT, or something else. Do not assume CGNAT from a mismatch alone. A WAN IP in 100.64.0.0/10 often indicates CGNAT; private WAN ranges usually mean another router in front. Ask your ISP about CGNAT / public IPv4 if inbound access fails. A VPN into home remains a valid fallback.

More detail: Remote WebDAV — public IP, NAT, and CGNAT.

DDNS works on mobile data but not on home Wi‑Fi

Many routers lack NAT loopback (hairpin). Use the local IP while at home, and the public hostname when away — or test remote access only from outside the LAN.
